# MuonPlayer Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 28 July 2026_

MuonPlayer collects no data.

The app plays music files you already have. Your library — the files, the tags
you edit, your playback history, and the index MuonPlayer builds of them — is
stored on your own device and is never transmitted anywhere. MuonPlayer contains
no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, and no tracking of any kind. It
never contacts a server the developer controls, because there isn't one.

## Last.fm scrobbling (optional)

MuonPlayer can send the tracks you play to [Last.fm](https://www.last.fm), a
listening-history service. This is off by default and entirely optional; the
player is fully functional without it, and until you sign in the app makes no
network connection to Last.fm at all.

If you choose to sign in, you do so with **your own** Last.fm account. Your
username and password are sent to Last.fm to obtain a session key, which is then
stored on your device; the developer of MuonPlayer never receives, stores, or has
any way to read your credentials or your listening history. What Last.fm does
with the data you send it is governed by
[Last.fm's own privacy policy](https://www.last.fm/legal/privacy).

You can stop this at any time by signing out in Settings, which deletes the
stored session key from your device.

## Deleting your data

Because nothing leaves your device, deleting the app removes everything
MuonPlayer holds about you. There is no account to close and no deletion request
to file. To remove data you have already sent to Last.fm, use the controls in
your Last.fm account.

## Children

MuonPlayer is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone,
regardless of age.
